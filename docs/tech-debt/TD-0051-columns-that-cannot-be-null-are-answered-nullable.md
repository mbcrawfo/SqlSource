# TD-0051 - The PostgreSQL describer answers nullable or unknown for many columns that cannot be NULL

## Problem

The realistic corpus, [`tests/DatabaseTests/Postgres/Realistic/`](../../tests/DatabaseTests/Postgres/Realistic/), holds a truth for each column of each case, written by hand from the SQL and the schema.  Of the columns that truth says can never be `NULL`, about 200, the describer answers 19 `true` or `null` on PostgreSQL 14 and 17 on 18, after parts A to E of the designs below and the reading of a scan's column.  It answered 94 and 95 when the suite landed, 81 and 82 after part A, 64 and 65 after part B, 53 and 54 after part C, 42 and 40 after part D, and 32 and 30 after part E.  On 14 the count holds the 17 of 18 and the two `EXISTS` columns of `exists-as-column`; `merge-returning` does not run on 14.  [`RealisticNullabilityNoiseTests`](../../tests/DatabaseTests/Postgres/Realistic/Describer/RealisticNullabilityNoiseTests.cs) holds the count of each major version, in its `Budgets` by the major, and fails when it moves either way; each column's answer is in the case's committed sidecar, `query.sql.json`, but for `two-columns-named-id`, whose models the generator refuses and which has none.

A column of no origin is answered by the walk from the plan's `Output` ([`PlanExpression`](../../src/SqlSource.Tool/Describing/Postgres/Walk/Expressions/PlanExpression.cs), [`NullabilityWalk`](../../src/SqlSource.Tool/Describing/Postgres/Walk/NullabilityWalk.cs)), as [expressions](../architecture/postgres-nullability.md#expressions) and [set operations](../architecture/postgres-nullability.md#set-operations) describe; what it does not prove stays `null` with `no-origin`.  The noise that remains is of these kinds:

- **A function or an operator outside the tables of [`TotalFunctions`](../../src/SqlSource.Tool/Describing/Postgres/Walk/Expressions/TotalFunctions.cs)**, or one over a type the tables do not hold: a domain's column, an enum's (`max(status)`), a range's (`upper(r.during)`), a cast of an enum, `a.status::text`, or of a domain.  None is read in a session whose search path puts another schema before `pg_catalog`.  Nor is a polymorphic name that another schema of the database holds a function or an operator of (`count(x)`, `array_agg`, `jsonb_build_object`, a range's constructor or its `*`, which the walk reads only where `pg_catalog` alone holds the name), nor an overload of one of those names that `pg_catalog` holds and no row of `CatalogOverloads` pins.  No column of the corpus is noise of this kind alone.
- **`IS DISTINCT FROM` and `IS NOT DISTINCT FROM`.**  Each calls the `=` the parser chose for its operands, and is `NULL` when that `=` is a user's operator that answers `NULL` for two values that are not.  The plan prints the test the same whichever `=` it is, so neither is read, though over a built-in `=` neither is ever `NULL`.  No column of the corpus is noise of this kind.
- **A form over `COALESCE`, `GREATEST`, `LEAST` or `CASE` of no known type.**  These are typed from their arguments that are proved of a known type, so an operator, a function, a cast, an aggregate or a subquery's value over one is read.  A form whose arguments are of two types, or of a type the tables do not hold (a domain's column), has no type, and what is computed over it is not read.  No column of the corpus is noise of this kind alone.
- **`EXISTS` and `IN` before 17, and an uncorrelated subquery beside a parameter.**  PostgreSQL before 17 prints every kind of subquery as `(SubPlan n)` or `(hashed SubPlan n)`, so an `EXISTS` cannot be told from an `IN`, which may be `NULL` (`exists-as-column` `has_orders` and `has_uninvoiced_orders`, on 14 to 16 only).  A subquery that names nothing of the outer query is an `InitPlan`, which before 17 prints `$k`, as the statement's parameter `$k` prints, so a `$k` from 1 to the number of parameters is not read; no column of the corpus is noise for one.  In a statement that may name `OLD` or `NEW` no subplan is read at all, since the old or the new row may print under an alias a relation of the subplan has.
  The sidecar of `exists-as-column` records PostgreSQL 18, which proves its two `EXISTS` columns, as 17 does.  18 is the first recorded major that proves them, so the case has `checkedFrom` 18, and `--check` and the comparison with the sidecar leave it out below 18, on 17 too, where they would agree.  No committed record says how 14 describes it, and only the noise and safety tests, which run on 14, check what 14 answers.  A record per major, or 14 proving the hashed subplan its `EXISTS` is planned as, would lift the limit.  Any other case that gets `checkedFrom` is added here.
- **A subquery of `min` or `max` that the planner reads from an index** (`SELECT (SELECT coalesce(max(x.id), 0) FROM big x)`, correlated or not).  For an indexed column of all but the smallest tables, and of a table never analyzed, even an empty one, the subquery's root is a `Result` over an `InitPlan` of a `Limit` and an index scan, and only an `Aggregate` root is read.  So the answer can change with the table's rows and after `ANALYZE`, and sidecars written on two databases can differ.  No column of the corpus is noise of this kind.
- **An expression the walk cannot place.**  A value a node takes from the side a join null-extends, which it prints in parentheses, is read as unknown, so a pulled-up constant there proves nothing, and neither does a cast of such a column, `(o.note)::text`; a node that prints no `Output` on a side a join extends may compute any value; and a form the root computes over a set operation is not read, since the root prints the first branch.  No column of the corpus is noise of this kind alone.
- **A window aggregate and an aggregate with `FILTER`, other than `count(*)`** (`running-total-lag-rank` `running_total`, `sum(ot.total) OVER w`).  A window's frame may be empty and the plan prints the window by its name, and a filter may leave a group no row; neither is read, even over a `NOT NULL` column.  An ordered-set aggregate, `WITHIN GROUP`, is not read either.
- **`VALUES`** (`values-statement` `column1`, `column2` and `column3`).  A `Values Scan` prints `"*VALUES*".column1` and nothing of what the rows hold.
- **A column of no origin through a `Subquery Scan` or a `CTE Scan` that another entry keeps unproved.**  The scan prints the subquery's columns in the order the query above needs them and the plan names none of them, so the column may be any entry of the scanned plan's root.  `ProvedThroughScan` in [`NullabilityWalk.Expressions.cs`](../../src/SqlSource.Tool/Describing/Postgres/Walk/NullabilityWalk.Expressions.cs) proves it when one entry at least is proved of a type that carries to the column's, and every other is too or is of a family of `SetOperationTypes` that cannot reach the column's type, which the call `casts` confirms.  It stays unproved beside an entry of its own family that may be `NULL`; for a column of a text type, beside any entry that is no proved text, since an inlined SQL function's assignment cast, which the plan does not print, makes any type a text; beside a domain's entry or one of no known type; over a scanned plan that holds a set operation; and inside an expression over the scan's column.  A scalar subquery's value, an `InitPlan`'s or a `SubPlan`'s, is not read through such a scan or in a subplan's `ANY` column, since the rule that reads it needs the column's type.  No column of the corpus is noise of this kind alone.
- **A set operation's column that part C does not answer.**  Part C answers a column of a `UNION`, an `INTERSECT` or an `EXCEPT` where the inputs the operation returns rows of prove it ([`NullabilityWalk.SetOperations.cs`](../../src/SqlSource.Tool/Describing/Postgres/Walk/NullabilityWalk.SetOperations.cs)).  It leaves:
  - A `UNION` branch that the planner keeps as a `*SELECT* <k>` scan, where another entry of the branch keeps it unproved as it does a `Subquery Scan`'s column.  The scan's columns print in an order the plan does not prove, so it is read as any scan is, and only a member of a 14 `SetOp`'s `Append` that carries the planner's flag is followed by position.  No column of the corpus is noise of this kind alone.
  - A set operation under a join or one that a node above computes over, a `GROUP BY` over a `UNION ALL` subquery's `count(*)` for one.
  - A branch's value of a type outside the three families of built-in types (`SetOperationTypes`), a domain's column for one, since the planner's cast to the result's type is not printed.
  - A nullable column that a branch's `WHERE` filters to values that are not `NULL` (`union-distinct-search-ordered-limited` `label`, `o.note ILIKE @pattern`).
- **A recursive CTE** (`recursive-category-tree` `id`, `name`, `path` and `depth`).  Its columns reach the result through a `CTE Scan` of a `Recursive Union`, which prints them in the order the query needs and names none of the CTE's columns, and they have no origin to find them by.  The reading of a scan does not reach them: `id` and `depth` share a family with the nullable `parent_id`, and `path` is computed from a column of the work table whose type the plan does not show, where `anynonarray || text` over a nullable column would print the same as `text || text`.
- **The ordinal of `WITH ORDINALITY`** (`unnest-tags-with-ordinality` `ordinal`).  It is a column of a function in `FROM`, which has no origin, and the plan prints nothing of `WITH ORDINALITY`: a `Function Scan` prints the call and the columns the query above needs, and nothing says which column, if any, is the ordinal.
- **Grouping sets** (`rollup-by-status` `orders` and `level`).  A plan with a `Grouping Sets` key cannot be used, so every column keeps the heuristic, though a `count` and `grouping(...)` are never `NULL` in any group, the empty one included.  `orders` is a `count(DISTINCT o.id)`.
- **A column of a view**, which is `null` with `view` (`view-joined-left` `account_id` and `name`).  PostgreSQL records no `NOT NULL` for a view's column, and the walk answers a column with an origin only through the catalog.
- **A `RETURNING` column of a value the statement writes**, which is the catalog's `true` for a nullable table column where the statement writes a value that is not `NULL` (`insert-select-returning` `note`, `update-from-aggregate-returning` `amount`, `data-modifying-cte-archive` `payload`).
- **An expression over the columns of a partitioned `ModifyTable` target**, in a `RETURNING` list that names none of them plainly.  The target is an instance of the partitioned table itself, and `relations` asks only for the relations the plan scans, so no row of the table is fetched and the reference is not proved.

## Why it exists

The describer answers a column from the catalog, through its origin, and from the plan's walk where a gate asks for one; an expression, a set operation's column and a column of a view have no origin, and the walk proves only what the text of the plan settles with the catalog rows it has.  Each kind above needs a fact that text does not show, or a call the describer does not make.  Noise is safe: a column the describer cannot prove is not null is nullable, and the epic's rule is that a false nullable is a nuisance and a false non-nullable a silent wrong value.  The suite of realistic queries was built to measure the noise before any work lowers it.

## Impact

Medium.  Nothing is wrong, but on realistic SQL about a twelfth of the properties that could be non-nullable have a `?`, each a `!` or a check a user adds by hand; it is still the most visible way the describer disappoints.  The count grows with every new case of a kind above.

## Proposed fix

[`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-15-postgresql-nullability-noise-design.md`](https://github.com/mbcrawfo/SqlSource/blob/b997875efe96a73842ac179c2cdd10779de5a8c9/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-15-postgresql-nullability-noise-design.md) took parts A to C: expressions from the plan's `Output` (`count(*)`, window rankings, constants, tests, `COALESCE`, `CASE`, `GREATEST`, `LEAST`), the walk's following of an entry under an outer join, and a set operation's column from its branches.  [`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-20-postgresql-nullability-noise-part-d-design.md`](https://github.com/mbcrawfo/SqlSource/blob/b997875efe96a73842ac179c2cdd10779de5a8c9/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-20-postgresql-nullability-noise-part-d-design.md) took part D: total operators, functions and casts of `pg_catalog` by exact signature, aggregates over a group that has a row, scalar subqueries, `EXISTS`, `ANY` and `MERGE_ACTION()` on 18, system columns, and `relations` asked whenever a column has no origin.  [`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-23-postgresql-nullability-noise-part-e-design.md`](https://github.com/mbcrawfo/SqlSource/blob/c1a960bba0e0e17cfd3ce891f33168f83c977d85/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-23-postgresql-nullability-noise-part-e-design.md) took part E: a call that asks which names another schema holds, `count(x)`, `array_agg`, `jsonb_build_object`, `json_build_object` and the ranges where `pg_catalog` alone holds the name, the type of `COALESCE`, `GREATEST`, `LEAST` and `CASE`, and `COALESCE`, `GREATEST` and `LEAST` over both sides of a `FULL JOIN`.  What each remaining kind would need:

- **Functions and operators outside the tables**: a row of `TotalFunctions` for each, read from its C function in the source of 14 and 18 and pinned by `Catalog_TotalFunctions_ArePgCatalogsOwn`, as each row is; a row of `CatalogOverloads` for an overload of a name it holds, pinned by `Catalog_CatalogOverloads_ArePinned`.  A polymorphic name that another schema holds has no fix, since the text cannot tell which function the query calls.
- **`IS [NOT] DISTINCT FROM`**: read only where no schema but `pg_catalog` holds an operator `=`, with `=` added to `CatalogOverloads.OperatorNames`, `CatalogNamesIn` reporting it for such a test, and every overload of `=` in `pg_catalog` pinned on 14 and 18.  That changes the request of `overloads`, so the fixtures are recorded again.
- **`EXISTS` and `IN` before 17**: nothing in the text of 14 to 16; the limit goes when 17 is the oldest supported major, or with a record per major.  **An uncorrelated subquery beside a parameter**: the same; nothing in the text of 14 to 16 tells `$k` from the statement's parameter.
- **A subquery of `min` or `max` read from an index**: a `Result` root over the `min` or `max` `InitPlan` read as one row, which needs its `One-Time Filter` read as a new plan key with a renamer kind, and the shape pinned in `PlanOutputTests` on 14 and 18.
- **A window aggregate**: the window's frame, proved to hold the current row; **`FILTER`**: none known.
- **`VALUES`**: the rows read from the SQL, for a statement that is a `VALUES` alone, since the plan prints none of them.
- **A column of no origin through a scan, a set operation's `*SELECT* <k>` branch and a recursive CTE**, where another entry keeps it unproved: the subquery's, the branch's or the CTE's column names from the server.  No catalog function or `EXPLAIN` option gives them.  `debug_print_parse`, which any user may set, prints them, but writes the query's tree to the server's log, so the describer does not set it.
- **A scalar subquery's value through a scan or an `ANY` column**: the column's type passed from `EntryProves` to `ValueTypeOf`, with tests that an entry that is an `EXISTS`, an `ARRAY` or, before 17, an `ALL` is not taken for a value of that type.  A subplan's `ANY` column has no type in the row description, so it needs another way to tell its value's type.
- **The ordinal of `WITH ORDINALITY`**: the scan's column names from the server, which no call asks for.
- **Grouping sets**: a plan with a `Grouping Sets` key used for a column of no origin whose form is never `NULL` in any group, `count(*)` and `grouping(...)`, while a column with an origin keeps the heuristic.
- **A view's column**: the walk proving the entry the plan prints for it, a column of the tables the view expands to, as it proves a column of no origin.
- **A `RETURNING` column of a written value**: the value the plan's `ModifyTable` writes into the column, proved as an expression, where no `BEFORE` trigger and no rule can change the row.
- **An expression over a partitioned `ModifyTable` target**: `relations` asked for the target of a root `ModifyTable` too, as for a relation the plan scans.

## Trigger

A user's report of a model with nullable properties that need not be; the tool's dropping PostgreSQL 14 to 16, for `EXISTS` there; a way to read a subquery's column names that writes nothing to the server's log, for a scan's column beside an unproved entry and a recursive CTE.
