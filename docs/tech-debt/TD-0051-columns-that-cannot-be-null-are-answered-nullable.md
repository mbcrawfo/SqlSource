# TD-0051 - The PostgreSQL describer answers nullable or unknown for many columns that cannot be NULL

## Problem

The realistic corpus, [`tests/SqlSource.Tests.Postgres/Realistic/`](../../tests/SqlSource.Tests.Postgres/Realistic/), holds a truth for each column of each case, written by hand from the SQL and the schema.  Of the columns that truth says can never be `NULL`, about 200, the describer answers 42 `true` or `null` on PostgreSQL 14 and 40 on 18, after parts A to D of the designs below.  It answered 94 and 95 when the suite landed, 81 and 82 after part A, 64 and 65 after part B, and 53 and 54 after part C.  On 14 the count holds the 40 of 18 and the two `EXISTS` columns of `exists-as-column`; `merge-returning` does not run on 14.  [`RealisticNullabilityNoiseTests`](../../tests/SqlSource.Tests.Postgres/Realistic/Describer/RealisticNullabilityNoiseTests.cs) holds the count of each major version, in its `Budgets` by the major, and fails when it moves either way; each column's answer is in the case's committed sidecar, `query.sql.json`, but for `two-columns-named-id`, whose models the generator refuses and which has none.

A column of no origin is answered by the walk from the plan's `Output` ([`PlanExpression`](../../src/SqlSource.Tool/Describing/Postgres/Walk/PlanExpression.cs), [`NullabilityWalk`](../../src/SqlSource.Tool/Describing/Postgres/Walk/NullabilityWalk.cs)), as [expressions](../architecture/postgres-nullability.md#expressions) and [set operations](../architecture/postgres-nullability.md#set-operations) describe; what it does not prove stays `null` with `no-origin`.  The noise that remains is of these kinds:

- **`count(x)` and a polymorphic signature**: `count(x)` in `any-ids-and-enum-array` `order_count`, `category-parent-self-join` `product_count`, `count-filter-statuses` `with_email`, `group-by-having` `order_count` and `json-build-and-agg` `order_count`; an `array_agg` in `array-and-string-agg` `product_ids`; a `jsonb_build_object` in `json-build-and-agg` `account`; and the range intersection `r.during * tstzrange(@from, @to)` in `reservations-in-range` `overlap`.  `pg_catalog` holds `count("any")`, `array_agg(anyelement)`, `jsonb_build_object(VARIADIC "any")` and `*(anyrange, anyrange)`, and a function or an operator of another schema whose signature is the argument's exact type beats a polymorphic one whatever the order of the search path, and prints bare.  So the text cannot tell the catalog's from a user's, and the walk reads none, nor a ranking with arguments.
- **A function or an operator outside the tables of [`TotalFunctions`](../../src/SqlSource.Tool/Describing/Postgres/Walk/TotalFunctions.cs)**, or one over a type the tables do not hold: a domain's column, an enum's (`max(status)`), a range's (`upper(r.during)`), a cast of an enum, `a.status::text`, or of a domain.  None is read in a session whose search path puts another schema before `pg_catalog`.  No column of the corpus is noise of this kind alone.
- **A form over `COALESCE`, `GREATEST`, `LEAST` or `CASE`.**  These are not `NULL` but of no known type (`TotalFunctions.UnknownType`), so an operator, a function, a cast, an aggregate or a subquery's value over one is not read: `sum(coalesce(x, 0))` in `cte-chain` `lifetime`, which is behind a scan too.
- **`EXISTS` and `IN` on 14, and an uncorrelated subquery.**  14 prints every kind of subquery as `(SubPlan n)` or `(hashed SubPlan n)`, so an `EXISTS` cannot be told from an `IN`, which may be `NULL` (`exists-as-column` `has_orders` and `has_uninvoiced_orders`, on 14 only).  A subquery that names nothing of the outer query is an `InitPlan`, which prints `$n` on 14 and `(InitPlan n).col1` on 18, and is not read on either; no column of the corpus is noise for one.  In a statement that may name `OLD` or `NEW` no subplan is read at all, since the old or the new row may print under an alias a relation of the subplan has.
  The sidecar of `exists-as-column` records PostgreSQL 18, which proves its two `EXISTS` columns, so the case has `checkedFrom` 18 and `--check` and the comparison with the sidecar leave it out on 14.  No committed record says how 14 describes it, and only the noise and safety tests, which run on 14, check what 14 answers.  A record per major, or 14 proving the hashed subplan its `EXISTS` is planned as, would lift the limit.  Any other case that gets `checkedFrom` is added here.
- **An expression the walk cannot place.**  A value a node takes from the side a join null-extends, which it prints in parentheses, is read as unknown, so a pulled-up constant there proves nothing, and neither does a cast of such a column, `(o.note)::text`; a node that prints no `Output` on a side a join extends may compute any value; and a form the root computes over a set operation is not read, since the root prints the first branch.  No column of the corpus is noise of this kind alone.
- **A window aggregate and an aggregate with `FILTER`, other than `count(*)`** (`running-total-lag-rank` `running_total`, `sum(ot.total) OVER w`).  A window's frame may be empty and the plan prints the window by its name, and a filter may leave a group no row; neither is read, even over a `NOT NULL` column.  An ordered-set aggregate, `WITHIN GROUP`, is not read either.
- **`VALUES`** (`values-statement` `column1`, `column2` and `column3`).  A `Values Scan` prints `"*VALUES*".column1` and nothing of what the rows hold.
- **A column of no origin through a `Subquery Scan` or a `CTE Scan`** (`cte-chain` `order_count`, `lifetime` and `last_placed_at`; `parameter-in-cte` `order_count` and `last_placed_at`; `update-from-aggregate-returning` `total` and `line_count`; `running-total-lag-rank` `total`).  The scan prints the subquery's columns in the order the query above needs them, not in the subquery's own, and the plan prints no name of the subquery's columns, so which entry of the subquery's plan the column is cannot be told; a column with an origin is found by that origin instead, which a column of no origin has none of.  Each is a `count(*)` or another aggregate, which the walk would mostly prove at the subquery's grouped `Aggregate`.  A subquery the planner flattens has no scan, and its expressions are read as the query's own, as `aggregate-cte-joined-inner`'s are.
- **A set operation's column that part C does not answer.**  Part C answers a column of a `UNION`, an `INTERSECT` or an `EXCEPT` where the inputs the operation returns rows of prove it ([`NullabilityWalk.SetOperations.cs`](../../src/SqlSource.Tool/Describing/Postgres/Walk/NullabilityWalk.SetOperations.cs)).  It leaves:
  - A `UNION` branch that the planner keeps as a `*SELECT* <k>` scan, for a cast to the result's type or a `LIMIT` (`union-all-activity-feed` `ref_id` and `at`, `union-distinct-search-ordered-limited` `id`, `union-mixed-types` `id` and `since`).  The scan's columns print in an order the plan does not prove: the planner pulls a `UNION ALL` that a subquery in `FROM`, a CTE or an inlined SQL function holds up into its query, and such a scan then prints the columns that query needs, in its order, by names the plan does not otherwise print; and on 18, where the planner drops its own leaf scan when it adds nothing, a `SetOp`'s input may be a user's subquery of the same name, through a view, an inlined SQL function or a `U&"..."` identifier.  Only a member of a 14 `SetOp`'s `Append` that carries the planner's flag is followed by position, since the flag keeps the planner's own scan there.
  - A set operation under a join or one that a node above computes over, a `GROUP BY` over a `UNION ALL` subquery's `count(*)` for one.
  - A branch's value of a type outside the three families of built-in types (`SetOperationTypes`), a domain's column for one, since the planner's cast to the result's type is not printed.
  - A nullable column that a branch's `WHERE` filters to values that are not `NULL` (`union-distinct-search-ordered-limited` `label`, `o.note ILIKE @pattern`).
- **A recursive CTE** (`recursive-category-tree` `id`, `name`, `path`, `depth` and `product_count`).  Its columns reach the result through a `CTE Scan` of a `Recursive Union`, which prints them in the order the query needs and names none of the CTE's columns, and they have no origin to find them by; `product_count` is a `count(x)` too.
- **`COALESCE` over both sides of a `FULL JOIN`** (`full-join-accounts-reservations` `account_id`, `coalesce(a.id, r.account_id)`).  Each row of a full join has one side at least, so a `COALESCE` of a `NOT NULL` column of each side is never `NULL`; the walk reads each argument alone, and a join extends each.
- **The ordinal of `WITH ORDINALITY`** (`unnest-tags-with-ordinality` `ordinal`).  It is a column of a function in `FROM`, which has no origin, and the walk proves no column of a `Function Scan`.
- **Grouping sets** (`rollup-by-status` `orders` and `level`).  A plan with a `Grouping Sets` key cannot be used, so every column keeps the heuristic, though a `count` and `grouping(...)` are never `NULL` in any group, the empty one included.  `orders` is a `count(DISTINCT o.id)`, a `count(x)` too.
- **A column of a view**, which is `null` with `view` (`view-joined-left` `account_id` and `name`).  PostgreSQL records no `NOT NULL` for a view's column, and the walk answers a column with an origin only through the catalog.
- **A `RETURNING` column of a value the statement writes**, which is the catalog's `true` for a nullable table column where the statement writes a value that is not `NULL` (`insert-select-returning` `note`, `update-from-aggregate-returning` `amount`, `data-modifying-cte-archive` `payload`).

## Why it exists

The describer answers a column from the catalog, through its origin, and from the plan's walk where a gate asks for one; an expression, a set operation's column and a column of a view have no origin, and the walk proves only what the text of the plan settles with the catalog rows it has.  Each kind above needs a fact that text does not show, or a call the describer does not make.  Noise is safe: a column the describer cannot prove is not null is nullable, and the epic's rule is that a false nullable is a nuisance and a false non-nullable a silent wrong value.  The suite of realistic queries was built to measure the noise before any work lowers it.

## Impact

Medium.  Nothing is wrong, but on realistic SQL about a fifth of the properties that could be non-nullable have a `?`, each a `!` or a check a user adds by hand; it is still the most visible way the describer disappoints, and `count(x)` and an aggregate in a `WITH` query are among the commonest forms of SQL.  The count grows with every new case of a kind above.

## Proposed fix

[`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-15-postgresql-nullability-noise-design.md`](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-15-postgresql-nullability-noise-design.md) took parts A to C: expressions from the plan's `Output` (`count(*)`, window rankings, constants, tests, `COALESCE`, `CASE`, `GREATEST`, `LEAST`), the walk's following of an entry under an outer join, and a set operation's column from its branches.  [`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-20-postgresql-nullability-noise-part-d-design.md`](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-20-postgresql-nullability-noise-part-d-design.md) took part D: total operators, functions and casts of `pg_catalog` by exact signature, aggregates over a group that has a row, scalar subqueries, `EXISTS`, `ANY` and `MERGE_ACTION()` on 18, system columns, and `relations` asked whenever a column has no origin.  What each remaining kind would need:

- **`count(x)` and polymorphic signatures**: a server call that tells whether any schema but `pg_catalog` holds a function or an operator of a name, from `pg_proc` and `pg_operator`.  Where none does, a bare name is `pg_catalog`'s whatever the argument types, so `count(x)` is a `bigint` that is never `NULL`, an `array_agg` of a group that has a row is not `NULL`, and `jsonb_build_object` and the range intersection, both total, are not `NULL` over values that are not.  It is a new call and a change to every capture of such a query.
- **A form over `COALESCE`, `GREATEST`, `LEAST` or `CASE`**: the type of such a form, the common type of its arguments, proved where each argument is of a known type of one family.
- **Functions and operators outside the tables**: a row of `TotalFunctions` for each, read from its C function in the source of 14 and 18 and pinned by `Catalog_TotalFunctions_ArePgCatalogsOwn`, as each row is.
- **`EXISTS` and `IN` on 14**: nothing in 14's text; the limit goes when 14 is no longer supported, or with a record per major.  **An uncorrelated subquery**: its `InitPlan` found by its name and read as a plan of its own, as a `SubPlan` is.
- **A window aggregate**: the window's frame, proved to hold the current row; **`FILTER`**: none known.
- **`VALUES`**: the rows read from the SQL, for a statement that is a `VALUES` alone, since the plan prints none of them.
- **A column of no origin through a scan**: a position the plan proves, a scan whose `Output` lists every column of the subquery, which its child's `Output` would have to show, or the subquery's column names from the server, which no call asks for.
- **A set operation's `*SELECT* <k>` branch and a recursive CTE**: the branch's or the CTE's column names from the server, which no call asks for.
- **`COALESCE` over a `FULL JOIN`**: a `COALESCE` whose arguments include a column proved on each side of one `Full` join, with nothing above that join that extends it.
- **The ordinal of `WITH ORDINALITY`**: a reading of a `Function Scan` that knows which of its columns is the ordinal.
- **Grouping sets**: a plan with a `Grouping Sets` key used for a column of no origin whose form is never `NULL` in any group, `count(*)` and `grouping(...)`, while a column with an origin keeps the heuristic.
- **A view's column**: the walk proving the entry the plan prints for it, a column of the tables the view expands to, as it proves a column of no origin.
- **A `RETURNING` column of a written value**: the value the plan's `ModifyTable` writes into the column, proved as an expression, where no `BEFORE` trigger and no rule can change the row.

## Trigger

A user's report of a model with nullable properties that need not be; the tool's dropping PostgreSQL 14, for `EXISTS` there; the branch's column names from the server, for a `UNION`'s leaf scan and a recursive CTE.
